    Electric water heaters are common in homes without natural gas access or where homeowners prefer electric appliances.     Attic and Closet Water Heater Installs in Lake Oswego

    Some local homes have water heaters in attics or interior closets. These installs require careful planning for access, drain pans and relief line routing.     Traditional Tank Water Heater Installation in Lake Oswego

    Most homes still rely on traditional tank water heaters for their hot water needs. These units store and heat a large volume of water, making them a dependable choice for families. Our installation service includes proper sizing based on your household's demand, providing adequate hot water even during peak usage.     Gas Water Heater Installation in Lake Oswego, OR

    Gas water heaters are a popular choice in Lake Oswego due to their efficiency and faster recovery rates. Our certified plumbers meticulously install gas lines, make sure of secure connections and verify proper ventilation to prevent carbon monoxide buildup. We also install required expansion tanks and check for gas leaks, adhering strictly to all local safety codes.

    Expansion Tank Installation in Lake Oswego, OR

    Many homes have a pressure reducing valve or check valve that creates a closed system. When water heats and expands, pressure can rise and stress pipes and relief valves. An expansion tank absorbs that extra pressure. We install and pre-charge the tank to match your water pressure. This protects your new water heater and pipes.

    Hybrid Heat Pump Water Heater Installation in Lake Oswego, OR

    Hybrid heat pump water heaters pull heat from surrounding air, which can make them efficient in warm garages and utility rooms. We install models from Rheem and A.O. Smith with proper condensate drainage. These units need adequate air space and clearance. They may qualify for utility rebates or tax credits. We help you decide if a hybrid is right for your home.

    Water Heater Installation Cost in Lake Oswego, OR

    Tank water heater installations usually cost less upfront than tankless systems. Gas tankless installs can cost more because of gas line upgrades and venting.
