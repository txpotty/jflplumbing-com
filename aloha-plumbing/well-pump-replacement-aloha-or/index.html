    Frequently Asked Questions About Well Pump Replacement in Aloha

    The cost of well pump replacement in Aloha, OR usually ranges from $1,500 to $3,500, though it can vary. Factors affecting the price include the type of pump, well depth, horsepower and any additional components needed like pressure tanks or wiring upgrades. We provide a detailed, upfront quote after a careful assessment.

    Shallow well pumps, or jet pumps, are designed for wells where the water level is typically 25 feet or less from the pump, using suction to draw water. Deep well pumps, or submersible pumps, are placed directly in the water near the bottom of the well and push water up, suited for depths over 25 feet. Choosing the correct type is critical for efficiency and performance.

    The lifespan of a well pump varies greatly depending on usage, water quality and pump type, but most last between 10 and 20 years. Regular maintenance and proper sizing can extend its life, but eventually wear and tear will call for replacement.

    Jet pumps sit above ground and pull water up from shallower wells, typically less than 25 feet. Submersible pumps are placed directly inside the well casing, submerged in water, and are used for deeper wells, pushing water up to the surface. Each has its own applications and benefits.

    Most standard well pump replacements can be finished within a single day, typically 4 to 8 hours. However, complex installations involving very deep wells, difficult access or additional system repairs may take longer. Our team works quickly to restore your water as soon as possible.

    Common signs of a failing well pump include no water, sputtering faucets, reduced water pressure, discolored water or surprisingly high electricity bills. If your pump runs constantly or cycles on and off too frequently, these are also signs that replacement may be necessary.

    We install Grundfos, Goulds and Franklin Electric pumps. These brands are reliable and widely supported.

    Regular maintenance, such as checking your well's water level, keeping the pressure tank air charge correct and fixing any leaks promptly, can extend your pump's life. Avoiding excessive water use and installing a good quality sediment filter can also reduce wear and tear on the pump.
