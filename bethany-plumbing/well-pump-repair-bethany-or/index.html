    Well Pump Electrical Troubleshooting in Bethany, OR

    Many well pump issues stem from electrical problems, including tripped circuit breakers, faulty wiring, or a malfunctioning control box. These can prevent the pump from starting or cause it to run erratically. Our licensed plumbers are also adept at electrical diagnostics, safely testing circuits, relays, and capacitors to identify and repair electrical faults. We make sure all wiring and connections meet Oregon electrical codes, providing a safe and reliable power supply to your well pump.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Bethany

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop. Pitted contacts, clogged sensing tubes and insect damage are frequent causes of failure. We replace the switch, clean or replace the tube and set the cut-in and cut-out pressures to match your tank. This is one of the most budget-friendly well repairs.

    Constant Pressure System Repair in Bethany, OR

    Variable speed systems like the Grundfos SQE and Franklin SubDrive keep pressure steady by adjusting motor speed. When they fault out, the drive typically shows a code that guides diagnosis. We troubleshoot drives, sensors and wiring and repair or replace components. These systems need a smaller pressure tank but correct setup.

    Jet Pump System Repair in Bethany, OR

    Jet pumps, usually found above ground, use an ejector assembly to draw water from the well. Common problems include air leaks in the suction line, clogged nozzles, or a failing motor. These issues can lead to intermittent water flow or a pump that runs continuously without building pressure. JFL Plumbing can effectively troubleshoot and repair these components, ensuring your jet pump system in Bethany operates at peak efficiency. We also check for proper priming, which is crucial for jet pump function.

    Well Pump Repair vs Replacement Cost in Bethany, OR

    Control and pressure switch repairs often cost $150 to $500, while a pressure tank replacement typically runs $600 to $1,500. Pulling and replacing a submersible pump can range from $1,800 to $5,000 or more, depending on depth, horsepower and wire. If your pump is older and already needs pulling, replacing it at the same time can save a second pull later. We lay out both options with clear numbers.
