    Frequently Asked Questions About Rooter Services in Gresham

    Rooting a sink, tub or toilet typically costs $125 to $300. Main sewer line rooting through a cleanout generally runs $200 to $500. Adding hydro jetting brings the total to about $450 to $1,000.

    Most standard rooter services for isolated clogs can be completed within 1 to 3 hours. More complex main sewer line blockages, especially those involving extensive root removal or hydro-jetting, might take longer, sometimes up to a full day. We aim for prompt service with lasting results.

    Tree roots are naturally attracted to water and nutrients, which they find in sewer lines through tiny cracks or loose joints. As roots grow, they can expand and completely block the pipe, a common issue in Gresham neighborhoods with mature trees and older pipes.

    Drain snaking, also known as augering, uses a long, flexible cable with a cutting head to physically break through or retrieve blockages like hair or tree roots. Hydro-jetting uses high-pressure water to clean the entire interior of the pipe, removing grease, mineral buildup, and sludge, leaving pipes much cleaner than snaking alone. Hydro-jetting is usually more effective for severe or recurring clogs.

    For most standard drain cleaning or rooter services within your property's existing plumbing system, permits are generally not required in Gresham, OR. However, if the rooter service reveals a need for significant repair, replacement, or excavation of the main sewer line, permits may be necessary. We will let you know if any permits are needed for your situation.

    We use industry-leading equipment for our rooter services. Our technicians commonly use professional-grade tools from brands like RIDGID, Spartan Tool, and Gorlitz for drain augers, sewer cameras, and hydro-jetting machines. These brands are known for their reliability and performance in tough plumbing situations.

    Rooting cuts roots back but does not seal the pipe joints, so roots eventually regrow. Root treatments slow regrowth, and pipe lining or replacement provides a lasting fix.

    Homes with tree roots or older clay pipe often need rooting every 12 to 24 months. Homes with newer PVC lines may go years without it. A camera inspection can help set the right schedule.
