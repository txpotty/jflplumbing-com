    Frequently Asked Questions About Grease Trap Installation in Oregon City

    The cost of grease trap installation in Oregon City, OR varies significantly based on the size, type, and complexity of the system. Small under-sink units might range from $1,000 to $3,000, while large outdoor interceptors requiring excavation can cost anywhere from $5,000 to $20,000 or more. We provide transparent, upfront pricing after a thorough on-site assessment.

    We install a variety of trusted grease trap brands, including Zurn, Big Dipper, Schier, and Rockford Separators. Our plumbers are familiar with the specifications and installation requirements of numerous manufacturers, ensuring you get a high-quality, durable system tailored to your needs.

    The cleaning frequency for a new grease trap depends on its size, the volume of FOG produced by your kitchen, and local regulations in Oregon City. Typically, grease traps require cleaning every 1 to 3 months. Regular cleaning is essential to maintain efficiency and prevent backups.

    We calculate flow from every connected fixture, including sinks, floor sinks and dish machines. Hydromechanical units are typically rated by gallons per minute, while gravity interceptors are sized by volume and retention time.

    Indoor traps should be close to the fixtures they serve and easy to open for cleaning. Outdoor interceptors are typically placed where a pump truck can reach them without blocking customer traffic.

    We install all common types of grease traps, including gravity grease interceptors, hydro-mechanical grease traps (automatic grease removal units), and compact under-sink grease traps. Our team helps you choose the best system for your commercial kitchen's specific needs and local code requirements.

    An indoor under-sink trap can often be installed in a day. Outdoor interceptors usually take several days to a week or more, depending on excavation, concrete work and inspection scheduling.

    Signs you might need a new grease trap include frequent drain clogs, slow drainage in multiple fixtures, persistent foul odors coming from drains, or if your current unit is old, corroded, or no longer meeting local code requirements. An inspection by a professional plumber can verify the need for replacement.
