    Dependable Sewer Line Replacement Services in Salem, OR

    Trenchless Sewer Line Replacement in Salem, OR

    To minimize disruption, trenchless sewer line replacement replaces pipes without extensive digging. We offer pipe bursting, where a new pipe is pulled through the old one, fracturing the old pipe outward. This is a great fit when protecting landscaping or driveways is a priority. The method works well for many homes in Salem and can often be finished faster than traditional methods, using high-density polyethylene (HDPE) pipe known for its long life.

    Cast Iron Sewer Replacement in Salem

    Cast iron drains in older homes corrode from the inside, particularly along the bottom of the pipe. Over time this leaves cracks, holes and a rough surface that snags waste. Replacing the cast iron sewer outside the home is often the first step. We can also evaluate under-slab cast iron and discuss lining or rerouting options. New PVC or HDPE will not rust or scale the same way.

    Sewer Line Material Upgrades in Salem

    Many older homes in Salem may have sewer lines made from outdated materials like clay or cast iron, which are prone to cracking, corrosion, and root invasion. We offer upgrades to newer, more durable materials such as PVC or HDPE (high-density polyethylene). These materials stand up to corrosion, roots, and shifting soil, giving your sewer system a much longer lifespan.

    Open-Trench Sewer Replacement in Salem, OR

    Traditional excavation is sometimes the best option, especially when the old line has a severe belly or the slope must be corrected. We dig along the line, remove the old pipe and set new PVC on compacted bedding at the proper grade. Rocky or hard-packed soil can slow digging, and we plan for that in the estimate. Each joint is glued and checked before backfill. The inspector sees the open trench before we cover it.

    Sewer Line Replacement Cost in Salem, OR

    Cost depends on length, depth, soil type, access and the method used. Open-trench replacement in an open yard can be reasonably priced per foot, while trenchless methods may cost more per foot but save on restoration. Hardscape, landscaping and driveways change the math quickly. We give you a written estimate for each option after a camera inspection. That way you can compare the full cost, not just the headline price.
