    High-Capacity Sump Pump Solutions in Redmond, OR

    For homes with serious groundwater issues or large basements, a standard sump pump may not be enough.     Discharge Line and Check Valve Installation in Redmond

    A great pump is useless if the water it moves runs right back toward the foundation. We route rigid PVC discharge pipe out of the basement and extend it at least 10 feet from the house or into a pop-up emitter or dry well. A spring or swing check valve keeps the column of water from dropping back into the pit after every cycle, which saves wear on the motor. Higher elevations and the eastern side of Oregon get much colder winters, where freeze protection is a regular part of home care. Where lines can freeze, we add a freeze-resistant outlet so ice cannot block the discharge.

    New Sump Pit Excavation in Redmond

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by locating the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor stays clean and level.

    Commercial and Multi-Unit Sump Installations in Redmond

    Office buildings, parking structures and multi-unit properties with below-grade levels usually need duplex sump systems with two pumps and an alternating control panel. The panel switches lead and lag pumps on each cycle so wear stays even, and a high water alarm alerts building staff. We coordinate electrical with your electrician and follow manufacturer specs for basin size and float settings. Property managers get a clear maintenance schedule to keep both pumps exercised.

    Sump Pump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in Redmond

    A complete sump pump system needs a properly installed basin and discharge line. We handle the excavation and placement of the sump basin, setting it at the lowest point of your basement floor so it collects water effectively. We also install the discharge line, making sure it is correctly sloped, sealed and routed away from your foundation to a suitable drainage area, so water does not re-enter your Redmond property.
