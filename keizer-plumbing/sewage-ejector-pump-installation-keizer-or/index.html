    Sump Pump vs. Sewage Ejector Pump Installation in Keizer

    While both sump pumps and sewage ejector pumps remove water, their applications are distinct. JFL Plumbing provides expert guidance and installation for both in Keizer. A sump pump handles groundwater and storm runoff, usually at a basement's lowest point to prevent flooding. A sewage ejector pump, however, is designed for wastewater containing solids from toilets, sinks and showers in below-grade plumbing. We make sure you get the correct pump for your drainage needs, preventing costly mistakes.

    Basement Bathroom Ejector Systems in Keizer

    Basement bathrooms sit below the building sewer, so toilets, showers and sinks drain into a sealed basin instead of flowing by gravity. We excavate the floor, set a basin usually 30 to 50 gallons and connect each fixture drain. A solids-handling pump lifts waste up and into the main drain line. The basin lid is sealed and vented to keep odors out.

    Common signs that you might need a new sewage ejector pump include the pump running constantly, not turning on at all, making unusual grinding or loud noises, or foul odors or sewage backing up into your basement fixtures.     A sewage ejector pump is designed to handle solid waste and wastewater from plumbing fixtures below the main sewer line, like toilets and sinks, discharging it into the sewer system. A sump pump, in contrast, removes groundwater from a basement or crawl space, only handling clear water to prevent flooding. They serve different purposes for different types of water.

    Pump replacements usually take two to four hours. New systems with floor excavation and venting typically take one to two days.

    A standard sewage ejector pump installation usually takes one to two days.
