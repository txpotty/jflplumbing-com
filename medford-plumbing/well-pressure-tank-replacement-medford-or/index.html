    Diaphragm Pressure Tank Replacement in Medford, OR

    Diaphragm tanks like the Amtrol Well-X-Trol use a fixed membrane that separates air above from water below. They are tough, but the steel shell can rust through at the base after years of condensation in a hot, damp equipment space. We replace corroded diaphragm tanks and check the stand and fittings under them. The new tank is leveled and supported so it does not strain the piping. This restores even pressure to every faucet.

    Pressure Switch Replacement in Medford, OR

    The pressure switch tells the pump when to start and stop, typically at 30/50 or 40/60 psi. Pitted contacts, insect nests and a clogged sensing nipple are frequent causes of failure in well houses. We replace worn switches with Square D or similar models and clean or replace the nipple below it. A low-pressure cutoff switch can also be added to stop the pump if the well runs dry. We set the switch to match your new tank's pre-charge.

    Tank Tee, Gauge and Relief Valve Replacement in Medford

    The tank tee is the manifold that connects the tank, switch, gauge, relief valve and drain. Galvanized tees corrode and choke flow, especially with sandy or mineral-heavy water. We replace them with brass or stainless steel tank tees that hold up much longer. A new liquid-filled gauge gives reliable readings, and a fresh relief valve protects against a stuck switch. These small parts make a big difference in how well the system holds up.

    Pressure Tank Sizing and Upsizing in Medford

    Many older systems have a tank that is too small for the pump, so the motor starts every time someone washes their hands. Tank size is based on drawdown, which is the usable water stored between cut-out and cut-in pressure. For example, a 10 gallon per minute pump usually needs at least 10 gallons of drawdown for a one minute run time. That often means an 80 to 119 gallon tank rather than the 20 gallon unit that came with the home. Upsizing is one of the best ways to extend pump life.

    When your well pressure tank shows signs of trouble, it is important to determine if a repair is feasible or if full replacement is necessary. Small air leaks or issues with the air valve might be repairable. However, if the tank itself is corroded, has a ruptured diaphragm, or is waterlogged, replacement is almost always the more cost-effective and reliable option.
