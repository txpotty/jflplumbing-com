    Frequently Asked Questions About Gas Line Installation in Bethany

    A simple indoor gas line for a range or dryer typically costs $350 to $1,000. Outdoor runs to a grill, fire pit or pool heater often range from $1,000 to $3,500 depending on distance, trenching and line size.

    We use trusted brands for gas line components, including Gastite and TracPipe for CSST, and high-quality black iron pipe from reputable manufacturers. We also install dependable shutoff valves and fittings from brands like Mueller and Nibco for durability and safety.

    Common materials include black iron pipe, which is very durable, and corrugated stainless steel tubing, which offers flexibility and can be faster to install. Copper tubing is sometimes used for smaller diameter lines but is less common for main lines. Our team selects the best material for your application and local codes.

    Materials like CSST and fittings carry manufacturer warranties, and we warranty our installation labor. We will go over the specific terms with you before work starts.

    Most residential gas line installations for a single appliance can be completed in a few hours to one day. More complex projects, such as extending lines for outdoor kitchens or multiple appliances, might take one to two days. This timeline does not include permitting and inspections, which can add several days to the overall process.

    Gas lines usually do not need frequent inspections once installed correctly, but it is good practice to have them checked during routine plumbing inspections or if you notice unusual smells or sounds. Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s may still have polybutylene pipe, which is prone to unexpected failures. For older homes in Bethany, an inspection every 5 to 10 years can help identify potential issues.

    A rotten egg or sulfur smell, a hissing sound near a line or appliance, and dead vegetation over a buried line are red flags. If you smell gas, leave the home and call your gas utility immediately.

    The most common sign of a gas leak is the smell of rotten eggs, which comes from an additive called mercaptan. Other signs include a hissing sound near a gas line, dead vegetation over an underground gas line or an unexplained increase in your gas bill. If you suspect a leak, evacuate immediately and call 911 and your gas company.
