    Pipe bursting replaces a badly damaged line by pulling a bursting head through the old pipe. The head breaks the old pipe outward while new HDPE pipe follows behind it. Bursting is ideal for collapsed or severely offset lines that cannot be lined. It typically requires an entry pit and an exit pit.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Trenchless Sewer Line Repair in Beaverton

    The cost of trenchless sewer line repair in Beaverton, OR can vary a lot, typically ranging from $4,000 to $15,000 or more, depending on the length of the pipe, the method used and the complexity of the job. Factors like pipe diameter and accessibility also play a role, so a professional assessment is key for an accurate estimate.

    Many residential lining and point repair jobs are completed in one day. Pipe bursting or longer lines may take one to two days.

    A rented snake may temporarily clear a clog, but it will not fix cracks, offsets or roots. Repeated DIY clearing can also damage fragile pipe, so a camera inspection is the better first step.

    You should consider trenchless repair if you have recurring clogs, slow drains, foul odors, lush patches in your yard or visible sewage backups. It is particularly helpful if your sewer line runs under valuable landscaping, driveways or structures you wish to preserve.

    Many cities require a permit for sewer replacement or lining, especially if work extends into the public right of way. We take care of permits when they are needed.

    Trenchless sewer line repairs are designed for the long haul. Cured-in-place pipes and new pipes installed via pipe bursting are very durable, often lasting 50 years or even longer. Many manufacturers offer warranties of more than 25 years on their materials.

    A properly installed liner has no joints for roots to enter, so root intrusion is practically eliminated in the lined section.

    For pipe bursting, we typically use high-density polyethylene pipe, known for its strength, flexibility and resistance to corrosion and roots. For CIPP relining, a felt liner saturated with epoxy resin, such as products from Perma-Liner or NuFlow, is used to create a new, seamless pipe within the old one.
