    New Sump Pit Excavation in Portland

    If your basement has never had a pump, we start by finding the low point where water collects. We saw cut the slab, excavate a pit roughly 18 to 24 inches wide and 24 to 30 inches deep, and set a perforated or solid liner depending on whether we are collecting groundwater or surface seepage. Gravel goes around the liner to help water flow in while keeping fine soil out. We then patch the slab flush with new concrete so the floor remains clean and level.

    Sump Pump Basin and Discharge Line Installation in Portland

    A complete sump pump system requires a properly installed basin and discharge line. We handle the excavation and placement of the sump basin, setting it at the lowest point of your basement floor so it collects water effectively. We also install the discharge line, ensuring it is correctly sloped, sealed and routed away from your foundation to a suitable drainage area, so water does not find its way back into your Portland property.

    Pedestal Sump Pump Installation in Portland

    Pedestal sump pumps are an excellent choice for narrow or shallow sump pits, as their motor sits above the water level. This design makes them easier to maintain and can often offer a longer lifespan because the motor stays out of the water. JFL Plumbing can properly install pedestal sump pumps in your Portland home, with the right sizing and setup for effective water removal without crowding your pit.

    Frequently Asked Questions About Basement Sump Pump Installation in Portland

    Replacing a pump in an existing pit usually runs $500 to $1,200 including the pump and check valve. Installing a new pit through the slab usually falls between $1,500 and $3,500, and adding a battery backup adds about $600 to $1,500.

    In many areas, including Portland, a plumbing permit may be required for new sump pump installations or major replacements to confirm compliance with local building codes. JFL Plumbing will help you navigate the permit process and make sure all work is up to code.

    Discharge should go to your yard, a dry well or an approved drainage area at least 10 feet from the foundation. Connecting a sump pump to the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed by local codes.

    Usually, standard home insurance policies do not cover a new sump pump installation or repairs unless the damage was caused by a sudden, accidental event like a burst pipe, rather than gradual wear and tear or groundwater seepage. Some policies offer separate water backup or sump pump failure endorsements for an extra premium. It is best to check with your insurance provider.

    Replacing a pump generally does not need a permit, but new pits, new discharge routes or new electrical circuits may. We check local requirements and pull permits when they apply.

    Swapping a pump in an existing pit typically takes two to three hours. A new pit with slab cutting and concrete patching typically takes most of a day.

    Most residential basements need a 1/3 or 1/2 horsepower pump, but the right answer depends on how fast water enters the pit and how high it must be lifted. We measure both before recommending a model.

    Watch for a pump that runs constantly, cycles on and off rapidly, makes grinding noises or never seems to turn on. Rust stains, a stuck float and water sitting above the switch level are also warning signs.
